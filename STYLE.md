# AirDash design direction

Utilitarian information design, with Bauhaus grid discipline and Palette typography. AirDash is an operational map and comparison instrument. The data relationship determines the layout.

- Keep the existing semantic air-quality colours; do not substitute decorative primary colours or a mood palette.
- Use Jost/Prompt and tabular numerals. Maintain readable contrast at every score level; clean readings must not fade into illegibility.
- Province names occupy one line, with urgency shown by the semantic colour marker; retain the full level in accessible labels and tooltips.
- Province comparisons use shared labelled metric columns. Numbers align to the right. Units and research limitations remain visible in the list heading and accessible row descriptions.
- Collapsed records size to their content, with44px minimum interaction targets. Do not add empty tracks, metric tiles or per-value boxes. Explanations open below the record at full width.
- Header information follows one datum in52px. Secondary tools belong in explicit Find/Menu drawers with keyboard focus and dismissal.
- The map owns the desktop operating area. Keep one intended scroller per panel; credits and installation information belong in About rather than after a fixed-height dashboard.
- Preserve Thai/English tasks and script legibility, unknown values, source dates and scientific qualifiers. A style name is not evidence of accuracy or usability.
- Verify populated collapsed and expanded records at320,390,770,1024 and1440px, in both languages. Check the actual image dimensions after resizing. A skeleton screenshot or an invisible ranking does not pass.

Reference vocabulary: UX Planet, “50 Design Styles Every Designer Should Know for Better Prompting”, sections38(Utilitarian) and22(Bauhaus). Composition and implementation rules: Dr Non’s Non-Slop and Palette. This direction does not protect existing unrelated legacy clutter from future repair.
